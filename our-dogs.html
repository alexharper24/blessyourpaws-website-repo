Reading Troy’s panel

The one thing her test found

Troy came back clear on 29 of the 30 conditions on the panel. On the thirtieth she carries one copy of a variant called CDDY, chondrodystrophy, and we would rather you heard that from us than found it yourself.

What it is. CDDY is the variant behind shorter legs, and it also makes the discs in a dog’s back age faster than usual. Dogs that have it are more likely to have disc trouble later in life. The panel puts that risk plainly: a dog with one or two copies is between five and fifteen times more likely to need back surgery than a dog with none. Plenty of dogs that carry it never have a day of trouble, and plenty of dogs with disc problems do not carry it at all.

What it means for a puppy. Unlike most things on a panel, this one only takes a single copy to matter, so it can pass from Troy to a puppy. That is the honest position, and it is why we are publishing the report rather than summarising it.

What we are doing about it. We are gathering our Cavalier sire’s panel now and will publish it here in full. Once we have it we will say plainly what it means for this pairing, and whether we are testing the puppies themselves. Hope to confirm before launch

Do not take our word for any of this. The full report is on Troy above, all fourteen pages, and the paragraph the panel itself writes about this variant is on pages 4 and 5.
