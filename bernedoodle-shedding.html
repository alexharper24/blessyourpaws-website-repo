Breed guide

Do Munchkin Bernedoodles shed?

Most Munchkin Bernedoodles shed lightly, some shed more, and no Bernedoodle is truly hypoallergenic. Where a dog falls depends on the coat that individual puppy grows, which is why we describe each puppy's coat to you instead of promising one.

Three breeds, three coats

Why one litter can grow different coats

A Munchkin Bernedoodle draws on three breeds, and each brings its own coat.

Poodle

The wavy to curly coat that tends to hold on to its hair.

Bernese Mountain Dog

A coat that sheds, and sheds heavily.

Cavalier King Charles Spaniel

A coat that sheds as well.

Every puppy inherits its own blend of the three, so two littermates can finish with noticeably different coats. As a rule of thumb, the curlier the coat, the less of it ends up on your floor.

The genetics

What decides a doodle coat

Researchers have found that a few genes explain most of the coat types seen in dogs, and each puppy inherits its own copies of them from both parents.

Length

One gene, FGF5, largely decides whether a coat grows long or stays short.

Curl

A second, KRT71, is behind the curl of the Poodle coat.

Furnishings

A third, RSPO2, produces furnishings, the longer eyebrows and beard that give a doodle its teddy bear face.

Furnishings matter for shedding too. A study of more than 4,200 dogs found that furnished dogs tend to shed less, that a separate gene, MC5R, also reduces shedding, and that dogs carrying both shed the least. A doodle that does not inherit furnishings grows what geneticists call an improper coat, with short hair on the head, face and legs.

What hypoallergenic actually means

There is no such thing as an allergy-free dog. The American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology is plain about it: no breed is non-allergenic, the proteins people react to are found in a dog's saliva and dander, and the length of its fur makes no difference.

Researchers have tested the idea directly. A 2012 study measured the main dog allergen in coat samples and in homes. Dogs sold as hypoallergenic, Poodles and Labradoodles among them, carried more of it in their coats than the comparison breeds, while the levels in their homes were no lower. The authors found no evidence for calling any breed hypoallergenic.

So if anyone in your home has allergies, the only reliable test is real time with a dog before you commit. Ask to meet ours on a visit.

Reading a puppy's coat

The coat you see at eight weeks is a preview rather than the final coat. The adult coat comes in over the first year, and its texture often changes partway through. We can tell you whether a puppy's coat is looking wavy, curly or straighter, and we will, but nobody can promise how it finishes.

Grooming

Less shedding means more grooming

A coat that holds its hair needs brushing, because the loose hair stays in the coat instead of landing on the couch, and it mats if it is left.

What to plan for

  • Brushing it through several times a week
  • A groomer roughly every six to eight weeks
  • Ideally, a groomer who knows doodle coats
  • Extra brushing while the puppy coat changes over to the adult one
